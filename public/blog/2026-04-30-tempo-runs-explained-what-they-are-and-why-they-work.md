# Tempo Runs Explained: What They Are and Why They Work

If you've seen "tempo run" on a training plan and wondered exactly what it means, you're not alone. A tempo run is a sustained, comfortably hard effort, typically held for 20 to 40 minutes, at a pace you could maintain for roughly an hour in a race setting.

![Runner maintaining a steady pace on a road during a tempo workout](/blog/assets/tempo-runs-explained-what-they-are-and-why-they-work/image_1.jpg)

Unlike interval training, a tempo run keeps intensity steady throughout. That consistent effort targets your lactate threshold, the fastest pace you can sustain before fatigue-causing lactate begins accumulating rapidly in your muscles. Lactate threshold pace is considered the strongest physiological predictor of distance-running performance, making tempo work one of the most valuable sessions you can add to your week.

## What Is a Tempo Run?

A tempo run, sometimes called a threshold run, is a sustained effort held at a "comfortably hard" pace for a continuous block of time, typically 20 to 30 minutes. The intensity sits firmly between easy jogging and all-out racing: hard enough that conversation becomes difficult, but controlled enough that you could hold the effort for roughly an hour in a race setting.

Unlike intervals, where you alternate hard surges with recovery, or fartlek runs, where pace shifts freely, a tempo run demands consistent effort from start to finish. That steady pressure is exactly what makes it effective.

## The Science Behind Tempo Runs

The physiological target of a tempo run is your lactate threshold (LT), the fastest pace you can sustain before lactate begins accumulating rapidly in your muscles and blood.

A landmark 1982 study by Bertil Sjödin and colleagues found that after 14 weeks of once-weekly 20-minute runs at threshold pace, trained runners improved their average pace from 5:43 per mile to 5:29, roughly a 4% gain. In practical terms, 10-mile race times dropped from around 57:10 to 54:50.

Training at or near LT pace teaches your muscles to clear lactate more efficiently and delays the point at which fatigue accelerates. Over time, your aerobic zone expands, and paces that once felt anaerobic become sustainable.

## Key Benefits of Tempo Running

Regular tempo work delivers a range of performance gains:

- **Running economy:** Your body learns to move faster using less energy.
- **Fatigue resistance:** You can hold harder efforts deeper into a race before slowing.
- **Pace control:** Tempo sessions teach you to feel and manage effort, which translates directly to smarter race pacing.
- **Mental toughness:** Sustaining a hard, controlled effort builds the focus and confidence needed on race day.

These benefits apply across every race distance, from 5K to marathon.

## How to Find Your Tempo Pace

You do not need a lab test to find a useful tempo pace. Three practical approaches work well:

1. **Pace-based method:** Target a pace roughly 25 to 30 seconds per mile slower than your current 5K race pace.
2. **Heart rate method:** Aim for 80 to 90 percent of your maximum heart rate, starting at the low end and letting it climb as the effort progresses.
3. **Effort-based method:** Tempo effort sits around 7 to 8 out of 10, comfortably hard but not a race-finish push.

You can also use Jack Daniels' VDOT tables to derive a threshold pace from recent race times.

## How to Structure a Tempo Run Workout

![Runner warming up with a light jog on a trail before a tempo session](/blog/assets/tempo-runs-explained-what-they-are-and-why-they-work/image_3.jpg)

Every tempo session should include a proper warm-up and cool-down. A standard format looks like this:

- **10 minutes easy jogging** to raise heart rate and loosen muscles
- **20 minutes at tempo pace** as the main effort
- **10 minutes easy jogging** to bring heart rate down

Before the easy jog, add 5 minutes of dynamic movements such as leg swings, hip circles, and high knees to prepare joints and connective tissue.

If a continuous 20-minute block feels too demanding at first, break the tempo segment into cruise intervals: for example, three 10-minute efforts with 2 to 3 minutes of easy jogging between each.

### Tempo Pace by Duration

Not all tempo runs look identical. Adjust duration and pace based on your current fitness and training phase:

| Duration | Relative Pace |
| --- | --- |
| 15 to 25 minutes | Slightly faster than LT pace (5 to 10 sec/mile faster) |
| 25 to 40 minutes | At LT pace |
| 40 to 60 minutes | Slightly slower than LT pace (5 to 15 sec/mile slower) |

In all cases, avoid starting too fast. Begin at the low end of your target zone and allow heart rate to rise naturally as the session progresses.

## How to Add Tempo Runs to Your Weekly Training Plan

Most runners do well with one tempo session per week. More than that without adequate recovery can lead to accumulated fatigue and diminishing returns.

A practical weekly structure pairs a tempo run on one day with a long easy run the following day, keeping all other sessions at easy or recovery effort.

- **Base-building phase:** Use shorter tempo blocks to build the habit and aerobic foundation.
- **Race-specific phase:** Extend tempo duration and add race-pace segments as your goal event approaches.
- **Taper phase:** Scale back tempo volume 2 to 4 weeks before your goal race.

Progress gradually, adding no more than 10 percent to weekly tempo volume at a time. [Trekon](https://www.trekon.run) offers structured running plans that integrate tempo sessions alongside easy runs and long runs in a balanced weekly schedule.

## Common Tempo Run Mistakes to Avoid

- **Starting too fast:** Going out hard spikes lactate early and blunts the training benefit.
- **Treating it like a race:** Tempo effort should leave you feeling you could continue, not depleted.
- **Skipping the warm-up:** Cold muscles increase injury risk and make the first tempo minutes feel unnecessarily brutal.
- **Progressing too quickly:** Jumping to long tempo blocks before building a base leads to burnout or injury.

## Tempo Runs vs. Other Speed Workouts

Understanding where tempo fits among other workouts helps you build a balanced plan:

- **Tempo vs. intervals:** Intervals use hard surges followed by recovery. Tempo holds a steady effort throughout. Intervals develop top-end speed; tempo builds sustained pace capacity.
- **Tempo vs. fartlek:** Fartlek varies intensity freely and informally. Tempo is structured and consistent.
- **Tempo vs. easy run:** Easy runs build aerobic base at low intensity. Tempo targets the threshold zone, a meaningfully harder stimulus.

[Runner's World](https://www.runnersworld.com/tempo-runs) notes that tempo runs fit year-round, while pure speed intervals are best reserved for race-specific phases.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Tempo Running

**How often should I do tempo runs per week?**
Once per week is the standard recommendation for most runners. Advanced athletes occasionally add a second lighter threshold session, but one quality tempo effort is sufficient for the majority.

**Can beginners do tempo runs?**
Yes. Start with shorter blocks, such as 10 minutes at tempo effort, and build from there. Ensure you have a consistent easy-running base of several weeks before adding tempo work.

**Can I do tempo runs on a treadmill?**
Absolutely. Set the treadmill to your target tempo pace and use a 1 percent incline to better simulate outdoor running resistance.

**Are tempo runs and threshold runs the same thing?**
Essentially yes. Both terms describe sustained running at or near lactate threshold pace. Some coaches use "threshold run" for the physiological concept and "tempo run" for the workout format, but in practice the two are interchangeable.
