# Yasso 800s: The Marathon Predictor Workout Explained

Few workouts in running carry the legend of **Yasso 800s** - a deceptively simple track session that claims to reveal your marathon finish time before race day.

Developed by Bart Yasso, former Chief Running Officer at Runner's World, the concept emerged from a pattern he noticed over years of training: the time he could run 800 meters in minutes and seconds consistently matched his marathon finish time in hours and minutes.

![Runner completing 800-meter intervals on a track during Yasso 800s marathon predictor workout](/blog/assets/yasso-800s-the-marathon-predictor-workout-explained/image_1.jpg)

The math is elegantly straightforward. If you can complete 10 repeats of 800 meters in 3:30 each, the theory suggests you're fit enough to finish a marathon in 3 hours and 30 minutes. Recovery between each interval equals the time of each rep, making pacing self-regulating throughout the session.

That said, the workout comes with honest caveats. Coaches and researchers note there's no hard scientific proof behind the prediction, and real-world accuracy can vary by roughly 15 minutes in either direction.

Still, runners and coaches alike continue building it into marathon training blocks for its clear practical value: it sharpens pacing discipline, builds speed-endurance, and offers a tangible fitness benchmark worth tracking across a training cycle.

## What Are Yasso 800s: The Marathon Predictor Workout Explained

Yasso 800s are a track-based interval workout where you run 800 meters repeatedly at a pace that mirrors your marathon goal time. The concept is simple enough to explain in one sentence, which is precisely why it spread so quickly through the running community.

### The History of Yasso 800s

Bart Yasso first shared the workout with Amby Burfoot, then Editor-in-Chief of Runner's World, who published it in 1994 and coined the name. Yasso had already been testing the pattern for years across his own racing.

Key details from the origin story:
- Published in Runner's World in 1994 by Amby Burfoot
- Named after Bart Yasso, former Chief Running Officer at Runner's World
- Personally validated across 15 to 20 of Yasso's own marathons
- Designed for accessibility - no lab testing, no complex formulas

## How the Yasso 800s Formula Works

The conversion is the entire secret. Take your marathon goal time in hours and minutes, then flip those units to minutes and seconds. A 3:45 marathon goal means you target 3:45 per 800m interval.

Recovery between reps equals the time it took to run each interval, making the pacing self-regulating. Build up to 10 total reps across your training block, starting with 4 to 6.

### Running Yasso 800s: The Marathon Predictor Workout Explained Step by Step

![Runner wearing a GPS watch on a track mid-interval, focused on pacing during Yasso 800s training](/blog/assets/yasso-800s-the-marathon-predictor-workout-explained/image_2.jpg)

A standard Yasso 800s session follows this sequence:

1. Warm up with 10 minutes of easy jogging
2. Run 800m at your goal time (e.g., 3:30 per rep for a 3:30 marathon)
3. Recover with a jog equal in duration to your interval time
4. Repeat for 4-6 reps when starting; build toward 10 over the training block
5. Cool down with 10 minutes of easy running

Effort level targets RPE 7-8 out of 10, roughly equivalent to 5K race effort.

## How Accurate Is the Yasso 800s Prediction?

### The Truth About Yasso 800s: The Marathon Predictor Workout Explained

The 800m repeats primarily stress VO2max and lactate tolerance - two meaningful markers of aerobic fitness. Marathon performance, however, depends on considerably more. According to [Runner's World](https://www.runnersworld.com/training/a71423575/yasso-800s-marathon-time-prediction/), the method provides a rough estimate within approximately 15 minutes but lacks solid scientific backing as a precise predictor.

What the workout genuinely reflects:
- Aerobic capacity and VO2max stimulus
- Lactate tolerance and speed-endurance
- Pacing discipline under controlled fatigue

### Who Gets the Most Accurate Prediction

Runners with a strong aerobic base and high weekly mileage tend to see the most reliable results. For them, the workout closely mirrors marathon-specific demands.

Speed-dominant runners face a different outcome. Their fast-twitch strength lets them hit target 800m splits that outpace their actual marathon fitness, so the prediction frequently overestimates their finish. Fueling strategy and late-race fatigue further separate real marathon performance from what a single track session can reveal.

## The Real Limitations of Yasso 800s as a Marathon Predictor

Completing 10 fast 800m reps on fresh legs says very little about how your body holds up at mile 20 of a road race. Aerobic endurance built over months of training is a different engine from the one powering track intervals.

Common scenarios where Yasso 800s mispredicts:
- Runners with low weekly mileage who lack the base for the full distance
- Speed-dominant athletes who are endurance-limited beyond 10K
- Anyone prone to fueling problems, carbohydrate depletion, or bonking late in a race

The workout was never designed to simulate tired-leg realities or race-day nutrition demands.

## The Training Benefits of Yasso 800s

![Runner finishing a Yasso 800s interval session on an outdoor track, checking pace data on a GPS watch](/blog/assets/yasso-800s-the-marathon-predictor-workout-explained/image_3.jpg)

Even with its predictive limits, the workout earns a place in any marathon training block. Yasso 800s deliver clear physiological value regardless of how closely your splits match race day.

- **VO2max stimulus**: repeated hard efforts push aerobic ceiling upward over weeks
- **Speed and turnover**: interval structure builds leg economy and faster cadence
- **Pacing discipline**: hitting precise splits on a track sharpens race-day awareness
- **Goal-setting confidence**: completing target 800m times makes your marathon goal feel achievable

## How to Add Yasso 800s to Your Marathon Training Plan

Yasso 800s fit best in the middle-to-late phase of a training block, once you have a solid aerobic base underneath you. Schedule them once per week and allow at least two easy days before and after.

A sensible progression across a 9-week build:
- Weeks 1-3: 4 reps at goal pace
- Weeks 4-6: increase to 6 reps, maintaining pace
- Weeks 7-9: build toward 8-10 reps as your peak session

## Frequently Asked Questions About Yasso 800s

### Can Beginners Do Yasso 800s?

Beginners can attempt Yasso 800s once they have a consistent base of weekly mileage and can run comfortably 4 to 5 days per week. Start with just 4 reps and use a conservative goal pace to avoid injury from jumping into hard interval work before the body is ready.

### How Often Should You Run Yasso 800s?

Once per week is the standard recommendation during an active training block. Schedule them in the middle weeks of your plan and phase them out during the final taper, when race freshness matters more than any additional fitness stimulus.

### What If My Yasso 800s Don't Match My Marathon Goal?

Treat the gap as useful data, not a reason for concern. If your current 800m times suggest a slower finish than your goal, you likely have a training gap in aerobic base, weekly mileage, or pace discipline. Use the result to either adjust your goal time or sharpen your training focus in the remaining weeks before race day.
