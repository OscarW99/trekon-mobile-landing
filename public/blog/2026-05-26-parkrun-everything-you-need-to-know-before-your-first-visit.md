# Parkrun: Everything You Need to Know Before Your First Visit

If you've been curious about joining your local parkrun but aren't sure where to start, this guide covers everything from registering online to crossing the finish line for the first time. Parkrun is a free, weekly 5km community event held every Saturday morning in parks and open spaces across the world, welcoming walkers, joggers, runners, and volunteers of all ages and abilities.

![A group of runners and walkers gathered at the start line of a parkrun event in a local park](/blog/assets/parkrun-everything-you-need-to-know-before-your-first-visit/image_1.jpg)

Getting started is simpler than you might think. You register once online, download your personal barcode, and simply show up. There are no entry fees, no dress codes, and no pressure to hit a certain pace. A tail walker ensures nobody ever finishes last.

Whether you're a complete beginner or a seasoned runner looking for a friendly Saturday routine, parkrun has something to offer. Read on to find out exactly what to expect before, during, and after your first visit.

## What Is Parkrun and Why Should You Try It?

Parkrun began on 2 October 2004 when Paul Sinton-Hewitt organised a simple 5K time trial at Bushy Park in southwest London with just 13 runners and five volunteers. That modest gathering has since grown into a global movement spanning more than 2,300 locations across 22 countries.

The format is straightforward: a free, weekly 5K held every Saturday morning in parks and open spaces. You can walk, jog, run, or volunteer - there is no competition and no pressure to perform. Research consistently links regular participation to physical, social, and mental health benefits.

For younger participants, **junior parkrun** offers a 2K event every Sunday morning for children aged 4 to 14, available in the UK, Ireland, and Australia.

## Is Parkrun Right for You?

There are no prerequisites. Parkrun welcomes complete beginners, regular walkers, people returning from injury, and elite runners equally. The minimum age is 4, and children under 11 must stay within arm's reach of a designated adult. There is no time limit, a tail walker ensures nobody finishes last, and dogs are welcome at most events. No booking is ever required.

### How Far Is a Parkrun and How Long Will It Take?

The distance is always 5K. If you expect to take longer than 30 minutes, start towards the back of the pack. Walking the whole distance is perfectly acceptable - many regular participants do exactly that.

## How to Register for Parkrun

Registration is free and only needs to be done once. Head to parkrun.org.uk, complete the short form, and you will receive a personal barcode linked to your athlete number. This barcode is valid at every parkrun event worldwide.

You can print your barcode, save it on your phone, or load it through the official parkrun app. No safety pins or race numbers are involved - just your barcode.

### How to Find Your Local Parkrun Event

Use the event map on the parkrun website and search by postcode or town name. Start times in the UK are 9:00am for England and Wales, and 9:30am for Scotland and Northern Ireland. Always check your specific local event page before travelling.

## What to Expect on the Day

### What to Wear and When to Arrive

Wear comfortable, weather-appropriate clothing and supportive shoes suitable for grass and paths. Aim to arrive 15 to 20 minutes before the start. A first-timers briefing takes place around 10 to 15 minutes before the run - this covers the course layout and local details. The Run Director then leads the main briefing for everyone.

### What Happens at the Start and During the Run

The Run Director counts down and signals the start with a klaxon. Timing begins from that horn, not from when you personally cross the start line. Faster runners position themselves near the front; if you are slower, move towards the back.

### What Happens at the Finish

![A volunteer scanning a runner's barcode at a parkrun finish funnel](/blog/assets/parkrun-everything-you-need-to-know-before-your-first-visit/image_2.jpg)

As you cross the finish line, you enter the finish funnel and receive a small plastic token showing your finishing position. Take this token to a volunteer scanner along with your barcode - both are scanned together to record your result. Do not leave the funnel before scanning, and do not take the finish token home.

Results are emailed to you the same day and include your finish time, age-graded score, and gender position. Personal bests are tracked automatically, and milestone clubs recognise runners who reach 50, 100, and 250 completions.

## Parkrun Volunteering

No prior experience is needed to volunteer. Contact your local event team through the parkrun website to find available roles. Common positions include tail walker, barcode scanner, marshal, and Run Director. Every volunteer role directly supports the sustainability of the event for the whole community.

## Tips for First-Timers

### Top Mistakes to Avoid

- **Forgetting your barcode** - your result will not be recorded without it.
- **Leaving the finish funnel too early** - wait until your token and barcode have both been scanned.
- **Taking the finish token home** - it is not a souvenir and is expensive to replace.
- **Arriving too late** - missing the first-timers briefing can leave you unsure of the course layout.

### How to Build on Your First Parkrun

Once you have completed your first parkrun, tracking your progress week by week is one of the most motivating things you can do. Consistent Saturday attendance combined with structured training in between is the most reliable route to improvement.

## Parkrun FAQs

![A smiling first-time parkrun participant collecting their finish position token from a volunteer](/blog/assets/parkrun-everything-you-need-to-know-before-your-first-visit/image_3.jpg)

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is parkrun free? | Yes, always - no entry fee, ever. |
| Do I need to book? | No booking required; just turn up. |
| Can I bring my dog? | Yes, at most events on a lead. |
| What if I don't finish? | No need to cross the finish line; just step aside. |
| How do I get results? | Via email after the event, or through the parkrun app. |
| Is there a junior parkrun? | Yes, 2K every Sunday for ages 4 to 14. |
| Can I run at a different location? | Yes, your barcode works at any parkrun worldwide. |

The most important things to remember are to register before you go, bring your barcode, arrive early enough for the briefing, and enjoy the experience at whatever pace feels right for you.
