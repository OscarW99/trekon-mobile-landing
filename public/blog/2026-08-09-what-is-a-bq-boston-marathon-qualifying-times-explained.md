# What Is a BQ? Boston Marathon Qualifying Times Explained

If you spend any time around serious marathoners, you will hear the term "BQ" come up constantly. A BQ, short for Boston Qualifying, is the official marathon finish time set by the Boston Athletic Association (B.A.A.) that you must achieve to be eligible to apply for the Boston Marathon.

![Boston Marathon finish line with runners crossing under a timing banner](/blog/assets/what-is-a-bq-boston-marathon-qualifying-times-explained/image_1.jpg)

These standards are not one-size-fits-all. The B.A.A. assigns specific times based on your age group and gender, measured using your net chip time from a certified marathon. Hitting your BQ does not guarantee a spot. Since 2012, Boston has used a rolling cutoff system, meaning the fastest qualifiers are accepted first, and runners have needed to beat their standard by several minutes just to secure entry.

---

## What Is a BQ?

A BQ, or Boston Qualifying time, is the official marathon finish time set by the B.A.A. that you must achieve to be eligible to apply for the Boston Marathon. Only net chip time counts, not gun time, and your qualifying run must take place at a certified marathon on the B.A.A. approved list.

---

## How BQ Standards Work: Age Groups and Gender Categories

The B.A.A. assigns qualifying times based on your age on race day and your gender. The minimum age to enter is 18, calculated as of April 19, 2027 for the 2027 race. Non-binary runners use the same standard as women. Your time must come from a certified marathon run within the qualifying window, typically an 18-month period before registration opens.

## The BQ Cutoff: Why Meeting the Standard Isn't Always Enough

Boston's field is limited to roughly 30,000 runners. Because far more people achieve a BQ than there are spots available, the B.A.A. ranks all applicants by how much faster they ran compared to their age-group standard. The fastest qualifiers fill spots first until the field is full. The point at which spots run out is called the cutoff, and anyone who beat their standard by less than that amount is rejected.

Here is how the cutoff has varied in recent years:

| Race Year | Cutoff Under BQ |
| --- | --- |
| 2021 | 7:47 |
| 2022 | No cutoff |
| 2023 | No cutoff |
| 2024 | 5:29 |
| 2025 | 6:51 |
| 2026 | 4:34 |

In 2025, a record 36,406 applications were received and 12,324 qualified runners were turned away. Given this history, simply hitting your BQ is not a reliable strategy. Aim for at least 5-7 minutes under your standard to cover most recent cutoff years. For 2027, runners who beat their standard but miss the cutoff are automatically entered into a random drawing for approximately 1,000 additional spots.

## The 2026 Standards Tightening: What Changed and Why

For the 2026 race, the B.A.A. reduced qualifying times by five minutes for all runners aged 18-59, the largest single adjustment in decades. Record demand had outpaced the existing standards, and average marathon finish times had improved significantly. Runners aged 60 and over saw no change. The 2027 standards match the tightened 2026 benchmarks.

## Course Elevation Rules That Affect Your BQ

Starting with the 2027 qualifying window, the B.A.A. introduced a net-downhill course policy. If your qualifying course has a significant elevation drop, the B.A.A. adds a time penalty of 5 to 10 minutes to your submitted finish time. If the elevation difference between start and finish is 6,000 feet or greater, the run does not qualify at all. Always check course certification and elevation data before choosing your qualifying race.

## How to Submit and Verify Your BQ Time

Registration for the 2027 Boston Marathon takes place September 14-18, 2026 through the B.A.A.'s online platform, Athletes' Village. All applications submitted before 5:00 p.m. ET on September 18 are treated equally, not first-come-first-served.

Steps in the process:

1. Run a certified marathon within the qualifying window at or faster than your standard
2. Log into Athletes' Village during the registration window
3. Submit your qualifying time and certified event details
4. Wait for verification, then receive confirmation based on the cutoff

## How to Train for a BQ

Chasing a BQ requires more than logging miles. A structured plan that builds progressively and targets BQ-specific pacing gives you the best chance of hitting your standard with a buffer to spare.

Key training principles:

- **Long runs**: build to 20-22 miles with portions at goal marathon pace
- **Tempo work**: weekly threshold sessions teach your body to sustain BQ pace under fatigue
- **Injury prevention**: consistent lower mileage beats sporadic high mileage

## Frequently Asked Questions About BQ Times

**Does a BQ guarantee entry into Boston?**
No. A BQ makes you eligible to apply. Actual acceptance depends on the annual cutoff.

**What counts as a certified marathon for BQ purposes?**
Any marathon on the B.A.A.'s approved list of certified events meeting distance and certification standards.

**What is the non-binary standard?**
Non-binary runners use the same qualifying times as women across all age groups.

**How often do BQ standards change?**
The B.A.A. adjusts standards at its discretion, typically in response to demand trends and improvements in average finish times.

**What happens if you miss the cutoff in 2027?**
Runners who beat their published standard but fall short of the cutoff are automatically entered into a random drawing for approximately 1,000 additional spots.
