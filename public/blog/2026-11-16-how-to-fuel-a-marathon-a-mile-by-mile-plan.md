# How to Fuel a Marathon: A Mile-by-Mile Plan

Learning **how to fuel a marathon: a mile-by-mile plan** could be the difference between crossing the finish line strong and hitting the dreaded wall at mile 20. Your body stores roughly 18-20 miles worth of glycogen, so without a consistent nutrition strategy, the final stretch can feel like a completely different race.

![Runner taking a gel at a marathon aid station while maintaining pace](/blog/assets/how-to-fuel-a-marathon-a-mile-by-mile-plan/image_1.jpg)

The science is clear: aim for 60-90 grams of carbohydrates per hour, starting within the first 20-30 minutes, well before fatigue sets in. Pair that with steady hydration and sodium replacement, and you give your muscles the fuel they need to maintain pace from start to finish.

But knowing the numbers is only half the battle. Every gel and sip of sports drink needs rehearsing on long training runs so your gut is ready when it counts. This guide breaks down what to consume, when, and how to adjust for your finish time.

## Why Marathon Fueling Makes or Breaks Your Race

When glycogen runs out, pace collapses and you hit the wall - fueling correctly makes this almost entirely preventable.

Key reasons fueling decides your race:
- Glycogen depletion begins silently, long before you feel it
- The final 6-8 miles become dramatically harder without consistent carb intake
- Gut training during long runs is what allows your body to absorb fuel at race pace

---

## How to Fuel a Marathon: A Mile-by-Mile Plan - The Core Strategy

The foundation is simple: target 60 grams of carbohydrates per hour as a baseline, rising to 90 g/h if you have trained your gut with both glucose and fructose.

Translate your hourly target into gel timing: space gels so the minutes between doses match the gel's carb content. A 25 g gel every 25 minutes hits 60 g/h; a 40 g gel every 40 minutes works with fewer stops.

Set your watch to buzz on your fueling interval, not on mile markers, so your rhythm stays consistent even as pace drifts in the late miles.

### Setting Your Carb Target by Finish Time

Your finish time determines how aggressive your carb strategy needs to be. Faster runners burn through glycogen more quickly and typically have better-trained guts from higher training volumes.

| Finish Time | Carb Target |
| --- | --- |
| Sub-2:30 | 90-120 g/h |
| 3:00-3:30 | 60-90 g/h |
| 4:00+ | ~60 g/h |

### Choosing Your Fuel: Gels, Chews, and Sports Drinks

Not all fuel sources are equal in convenience and gut tolerance. Test every product on training runs before race day.

- **Gels (25 g)**: faster to consume, ideal at crowded aid stations
- **Gels (40 g)**: fewer stops needed, but require more water to digest comfortably
- **Chews**: slower to eat, so spread them across two fueling stops
- **Sports drinks**: useful when gels become hard to swallow in the final miles

## Pre-Race Fueling: The 48-Hour Window

Great marathon nutrition starts two days before the race. Increase carbs to 8-10 g/kg per day using easy-to-digest sources: white rice, pasta, bread, and bananas. Reduce fiber to minimize GI distress on race morning.

On race morning, 2-3 hours before the gun, eat a familiar carbohydrate-focused meal of 1-2 g/kg body weight:
- Bagel with peanut butter and a banana
- Oatmeal with honey and berries
- Rice with eggs

About 30-45 minutes before the start, top up with a banana or gel and add 200-400 mg of sodium. If you use caffeine, take 3-6 mg/kg roughly 60 minutes before the gun.

## How to Fuel a Marathon: A Mile-by-Mile Plan Broken Down by Race Section

This is where strategy becomes action. The goal across all three phases is consistency - never let fueling slip, even when you feel great.

### Miles 1-10: Start Early, Fuel Before You Need It

Take your first gel around the 20-30 minute mark, well before any sense of fatigue. Target 30-60 g/h across this stretch to establish your rhythm without overwhelming your gut early.

At every aid station, drink to thirst rather than on a fixed schedule, aiming for 400-800 ml of fluids per hour. If it is warm or you are a heavy sweater, choose options with 100-200 mg of sodium per serving.

### Miles 10-20: Maintain the Rhythm

![Runner sipping sports drink and taking a gel at a mile 15 water station](/blog/assets/how-to-fuel-a-marathon-a-mile-by-mile-plan/image_2.jpg)

This is the stretch where discipline matters most. The miles feel manageable, which tempts many runners to skip a gel - resist that urge.

- Maintain 30-60 g/h of carbohydrates; swap gels for chews or sports drinks to vary texture
- Introduce caffeine gels around miles 13-18 if you have trained with them
- Keep electrolytes consistent: cramping risk rises as sweat accumulates
- Let your watch buzz every 30-45 minutes to stay on schedule

### Miles 20-26.2: Push Through the Wall

Never skip fueling here, even if your stomach protests. Small doses still help maintain pace. If gels are hard to swallow, switch to a sports drink. Keep dosing up to around mile 24.

---

## Hydration and Sodium: The Two Levers You Can't Ignore

Fluids and sodium matter as much as carbs. Target 300-600 ml per hour, adjusted for heat and individual sweat rate.

Drinking only water risks hyponatremia - dangerously low blood sodium caused by dilution. Always pair fluids with sodium:
- Aim for 200-500 mg of sodium per hour as a standard target
- Heavy sweaters (those who see white salt marks on their kit after long runs) need the upper end
- Alternate water and sports drink at aid stations to help dilute gels without over-hydrating

## How to Build Your Fueling Plan in Training

![Runner practicing gel intake during an 18-mile long training run](/blog/assets/how-to-fuel-a-marathon-a-mile-by-mile-plan/image_3.jpg)

Your race-day fueling plan is only as reliable as the number of times you have rehearsed it. Use long runs of 18-22 miles in the final 6-8 weeks of training as full fueling dress rehearsals.

Gut training rules to follow:
- Fuel at race pace, not during walk breaks - your gut needs to handle carbs under the same conditions it will face on race day
- Use the exact same gels, chews, and sports drinks you will use in the race
- Hit the same intervals, set your watch, and do not skip doses
- Never introduce anything new on race day

## Sample Fueling Schedule by Finish Time

Here is how to translate your target finish time into a concrete gel schedule:

| Finish Time | Gel Size | Interval | Total Gels | Approx. Carbs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3:30 | 25 g | Every 25 min | ~8 gels | ~200 g |
| 4:30 | 40 g | Every 40 min | ~7 gels | ~280 g |

Commit to a specific plan before race morning - never make fueling decisions on the fly.

## Post-Race Recovery Fueling

What you consume in the first 30-45 minutes after finishing matters. The recovery window is when muscles are most receptive to glycogen replenishment and protein synthesis.

Target within 30-45 minutes of crossing the line:
- 15-30 g of protein to support muscle repair
- 60 g or more of carbohydrates to begin restoring glycogen
- An electrolyte drink to start active rehydration

Good options: chocolate milk, Greek yogurt with fruit, or a banana-and-yogurt smoothie. Rehydrate steadily over the next few hours rather than drinking a large volume all at once.
