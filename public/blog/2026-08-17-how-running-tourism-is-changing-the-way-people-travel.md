# How Running Tourism Is Changing the Way People Travel

Not long ago, a vacation meant sightseeing from a tour bus or relaxing on a beach. Today, a growing number of travelers are lacing up their shoes and letting their legs lead the way. **How running tourism is changing the way people travel** is one of the most compelling stories in the modern travel industry, as runners increasingly plan entire trips around races, guided trail runs, and fitness-focused itineraries.

![Runners exploring a scenic city route during a running tourism trip](/blog/assets/how-running-tourism-is-changing-the-way-people-travel/image_1.jpg)

The numbers back this up. Accor reports a 50% rise in searches for "workout holidays," while Strava recorded a 59% jump in global running club participation in 2024. Run-trip bookings have surged 60% year-over-year according to Hana Tour, and TripAdvisor's 2026 Trendcast officially named "sweat-jetting" one of travel's defining trends. From marathon weekends in Cape Town to trail runcations through the Dolomites, running has become a primary reason people choose a destination.

## What Is Running Tourism and Why Is It Taking Off?

Running tourism goes by several names - runcation, race-cation, sweat-jetting - but the core idea is simple: choosing a destination because of running, not just bringing your shoes along for the ride. A runcation might mean spending a week trail running through Madeira. A race-cation anchors the entire trip around a marathon entry.

The pandemic played a decisive role in accelerating this shift. When gyms closed, millions turned to running for fitness and stress relief. Social media and the explosion of run clubs kept that momentum going, and fitness influencers began documenting race travels, turning events in Budapest, Cape Town, and Tokyo into aspirational destinations. Running tourism is no longer a niche pursuit - it is a mainstream travel category with its own booking patterns, hotel packages, and dedicated operators.

## How Running Tourism Is Reshaping Global Travel

The Abbott World Marathon Majors circuit has long shaped global running travel, drawing competitors to Tokyo, Berlin, London, Chicago, New York, and Boston. The addition of the Sanlam Cape Town Marathon as the circuit's first African member signals how far this globalization has reached, with tourism officials already anticipating a surge in international arrivals for 2027.

The economic logic is compelling. The Chicago Marathon draws more than 300% more visitors than a typical weekend. Destinations have taken notice, strategically scheduling races during shoulder seasons to fill hotels that would otherwise sit at low occupancy. Major race weekends can generate hundreds of millions in economic activity, with relatively low infrastructure costs since courses run on public streets.

### From City Races to Remote Trails

City marathons remain popular, but a growing segment of running tourists is looking beyond urban finish lines. Europe has become a hotbed for trail runcations, with Madeira's levada paths, Italy's Dolomites, and Germany's Black Forest attracting runners seeking scenery over city streets. Beyond Europe, runners are chasing experiences in AlUla, along the Great Wall of China, across Iceland's volcanic landscapes, and through Kenya's highland training grounds.

Research from Airbnb and Strava found that **74% of young runners** have planned or are considering a countryside runcation, driven by "city loop fatigue." More than half prefer countryside routes over urban ones, citing scenery, air quality, and crowd levels.

### The Race-cation Effect

A race entry is rarely just a race entry anymore. Runners typically arrive two to five days before race day and stay one to three days after, turning a single event into a five-to-eight night tourism window. Hotels, tour operators, and destination marketing boards have built entire programming strategies around this multiplier effect. The 2026 London Marathon received **1.1 million applications** for a limited number of spots, illustrating just how far race-cation culture has penetrated mainstream travel planning.

## How the Travel Industry Is Responding

### Hotels and Run-Friendly Packages

The hospitality industry has moved quickly to meet runner demand. Westin offers curated circuits and gear lending. Grand Josun Jeju launched a package including running route maps, detergent for workout wear, and smartwatch rentals. Conrad New York has integrated running experiences into its guest programming through a partnership with Go! Running Tours.

### Tour Operators and Guided Running Experiences

![Small group running with a local guide through a vibrant neighborhood market](/blog/assets/how-running-tourism-is-changing-the-way-people-travel/image_2.jpg)

Specialized operators have built entire businesses around the running tourist. Go! Running Tours now connects travelers with local running guides in over **70 cities worldwide**, leading small groups through neighborhoods that a tour bus would never reach. Marathon Tours & Travel and Rogue Expeditions offer multiday itineraries combining race participation with cultural programming. Contiki has launched run-club itineraries targeting 18-to-35 travelers across multi-destination European routes.

As [National Geographic notes](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/running-vacations-are-next-big-travel-trend), running vacations are the next big travel trend precisely because they blend fitness, community, and authentic discovery in ways that traditional sightseeing cannot replicate.

## Who Is the Modern Running Tourist?

Running tourism draws a diverse global audience, but some patterns are clear. **Women aged 25 to 34** represent the fastest-growing segment. Events like the AlUla Duathlon attract participants from more than **30 nationalities**, reflecting the genuinely global reach of this travel category.

Motivations vary, but several themes recur consistently:

- **Community**: shared trails and group runs create social bonds faster than almost any other travel format
- **Personal milestones**: completing a marathon in a new country carries meaning that a beach holiday rarely matches
- **Immersive discovery**: running through a city's neighborhoods offers access that tourist routes never provide
- **Accountability**: training plans and race calendars give travel a structure that appeals to goal-oriented travelers

## Planning Your Own Runcation

![Runner checking a route map on a phone while standing at a trailhead in a scenic destination](/blog/assets/how-running-tourism-is-changing-the-way-people-travel/image_3.jpg)

Planning a runcation requires a few decisions that a standard vacation does not. Start with these considerations:

- **Race calendar first**: choose your destination based on event timing, then build the itinerary around it
- **Shoulder season advantage**: many destination races are scheduled during off-peak periods, meaning better hotel rates and fewer crowds
- **Pre- and post-race days**: build in two to three days before the race for acclimatization and two to three days after for recovery and exploration
- **Guided vs. self-guided**: guided running tours add cultural depth; self-guided routes offer flexibility
- **Hotel proximity**: choose accommodation within easy reach of the start line and key running routes
- **Cultural add-ons**: museums, food markets, and day trips integrate naturally into non-running days

### The Future of Running Tourism

Running tourism is on a trajectory that closely mirrors the arc of golf tourism: from niche hobby travel to a recognized, commercially significant genre with dedicated infrastructure, specialist operators, and destination investment.

Destinations are already competing on race experience and fitness infrastructure as much as on culture or scenery. AlUla's wellness festival model, pairing athletic events with broader programming, points toward a future where fitness-led itineraries become a standard offering rather than a specialty product.

Younger travelers are driving this shift. Their priorities - active experiences, community connection, personal achievement, and authentic discovery - align naturally with what running tourism delivers. With booking surges continuing and new destinations entering the race calendar every year, the movement shows no signs of slowing down.
