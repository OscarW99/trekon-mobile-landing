# Hip Pain When Running: What It Usually Means

According to research published in the Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy, up to 11 percent of runners experience hip pain during training, making it one of the more common complaints after knee discomfort.

![Runner holding their hip during a training run, showing signs of hip pain](/blog/assets/hip-pain-when-running-what-it-usually-means/image_1.jpg)

The hip is a ball-and-socket joint subjected to repetitive loading with every stride. Small errors in training volume, recovery, or mechanics can quickly add up, and most running-related hip pain traces back to overuse rather than a single traumatic event.

Pain felt deep in the groin typically points to something within the joint itself, such as a labral tear or impingement. Outer hip or buttock discomfort more often involves soft tissues like tendons or bursae.

Understanding these patterns helps runners make smarter decisions about when to modify training and when to seek professional care.

## What Is Hip Pain When Running?

Running-related hip pain can originate from several structures:

- Tendons and bursae surrounding the joint
- The hip labrum, the cartilage ring lining the socket
- The femoral head and acetabulum (the ball and socket bones themselves)
- Muscles such as the glutes, hip flexors, and IT band

## Why Does Hip Pain When Running Happen? Common Causes Explained

Most running-related hip pain has a clear training trigger: sudden mileage increases, terrain changes, and skipped rest days. Muscle imbalances shift load onto structures not built for it, and poor mechanics compound the problem over weeks.

### Outer Hip and Buttock Pain: IT Band Syndrome, Bursitis, and Gluteal Tendinopathy

The iliotibial band creates friction where it crosses the hip, while the trochanteric bursa can swell with repetitive loading. Gluteal tendinopathy, felt as stiffness at the lateral hip, is especially common in women and often worsens after prolonged sitting.

### Groin and Front-of-Hip Pain: Hip Flexor Strain, Labral Tears, and Impingement

The iliopsoas group can strain under sudden acceleration or high training volume. Deeper groin pain tends to come from within the joint: labral tears or femoroacetabular impingement (FAI) produce an ache that worsens with hip flexion and prolonged sitting.

### Deep or Diffuse Hip Pain: Stress Fractures and Referred Pain

Stress fractures of the femoral neck are less common but more serious, disproportionately affecting female runners with high training loads or low bone density.

Hip osteoarthritis causes gradually worsening groin pain and morning stiffness. The lower back and sacroiliac joint can also refer pain into the buttock, closely mimicking local hip conditions.

## How to Tell Where Your Hip Pain Is Coming From

![Anatomical diagram of the hip highlighting outer hip, groin, and buttock pain zones for runners](/blog/assets/hip-pain-when-running-what-it-usually-means/image_2.jpg)

Pain location is the starting point for self-assessment. Clicking or locking adds useful context: clicking paired with pain can indicate labral involvement.

### Hip Pain When Running: What It Usually Means Based on Location

| Pain Location | Likely Cause |
| --- | --- |
| Inside hip or groin | Joint issue: labral tear, FAI, or arthritis |
| Outer hip or thigh | Soft tissue: IT band syndrome, bursitis, or gluteal tendinopathy |
| Front of hip | Hip flexor strain or impingement |
| Buttock | Spine, sacroiliac joint, or referred pain |

Referred pain from the lower back can travel all the way to the knee, which is why assessing hip pain in isolation sometimes leads to the wrong conclusion.

### Red Flags: When Hip Pain While Running Needs Urgent Attention

Stop running and seek prompt medical care if you notice any of the following, in line with [NHS guidance on hip pain](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/hip-pain/):

- Inability to bear weight after a fall or sudden impact (possible fracture)
- A hot, swollen joint combined with fever or chills (possible infection)
- Severe or rapidly worsening pain that does not settle with rest
- Pain that disrupts sleep or prevents normal daily activities

## How Hip Pain When Running Is Treated

Most running-related hip pain resolves without surgery. The core approach is reducing load on the injured structure while keeping surrounding muscles active, since complete rest can allow tendons to weaken further.

### Conservative Treatment: What Works for Most Runners

Physical therapy is the cornerstone of recovery. Key strategies include:

- Load management: cutting mileage, avoiding hills, and switching to lower-impact cross-training
- Targeted strength work for the glutes, hip abductors, and deep core
- Running gait assessment to correct overstriding or pelvic drop
- Progressive return-to-running based on pain response rather than fixed timelines

### When to See a Doctor or Physio

Book an appointment if:

- Pain persists beyond two weeks despite relative rest
- Pain wakes you at night or limits everyday tasks like climbing stairs
- You suspect a stress fracture, labral tear, or FAI, as imaging is needed to confirm these

## How to Prevent Hip Pain When Running

![Runner performing a single-leg glute bridge on a mat, demonstrating a hip strengthening exercise for runners](/blog/assets/hip-pain-when-running-what-it-usually-means/image_3.jpg)

Prevention comes down to two principles: manage training load sensibly and keep the muscles around the hip strong enough to handle it. Well-cushioned shoes and softer surfaces also reduce cumulative stress during high-volume training blocks.

### Building a Smarter Training Plan to Protect Your Hips

Gradual progression is the most effective tool against overuse injury. [Trekon](https://www.trekon.run) builds personalised training plans applying evidence-based progression principles, helping runners avoid the load spikes that drive most hip injuries.

Practical training habits to protect your hips:

- Limit mileage increases to roughly 10 percent per week
- Schedule at least one full rest or active recovery day each week
- Complement run sessions with cross-training such as swimming or cycling during heavy build phases

### Key Exercises to Strengthen Hips for Running

Weak glutes and hip abductors allow pelvic drop with each step, increasing stress on the IT band, labrum, and outer hip structures. Prioritise these movements:

- Glute bridges and single-leg glute bridges for posterior chain activation
- Clamshells and lateral band walks for hip abductor strength
- Hip flexor and quad stretches held 30 to 60 seconds after each run
- Single-leg balance drills to improve stance-phase stability and biomechanics

## Quick Reference: Hip Pain Causes at a Glance

| Pain Location | Common Cause | Self-Manage? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outer hip or thigh | IT band syndrome, bursitis, gluteal tendinopathy | Yes, with load reduction |
| Front of hip or groin | Hip flexor strain, labral tear, FAI | Mild strain: yes; persistent: see a clinician |
| Buttock | Referred pain from spine or sacroiliac joint | Improving: yes; worsening: seek help |
| Groin with weight-bearing pain | Stress fracture | No - seek urgent evaluation |
| Hip with fever or swelling | Infection (septic arthritis) | No - seek urgent evaluation |

Location guides diagnosis while load management drives recovery. Address the training error, strengthen the surrounding muscles, and most runners can return to full training within weeks.
